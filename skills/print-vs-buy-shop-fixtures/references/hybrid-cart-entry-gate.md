# Hybrid cart entry gate

Use this gate before escalating from clips/buy to a custom mobile shop cart.

1. **Verify the real buy price.** Compare delivered cost and local quote; do not anchor on a catalog price that omits freight, membership, tax, or local markup.
2. **Test the static-mount path.** Prefer small desk-wing or wall L-slides when the mounting surface, sit/stand motion, drawers, and knee clearance make that safe.
3. **Require an explicit cart pivot.** Enter hybrid-cart design only when the user rejects the verified buy price or needs a custom footprint. Record the user override in `docs/DESIGN.md`.
4. **Keep the structural ban narrow.** “Never cages” means never print long structural furniture members merely to claim an all-printed rack. It does **not** prohibit a functional four-post cart built from commodity stock plus printed joinery.
5. **Preserve the commercial architecture.** Four posts, open end-loading, paired side ledges, top/bottom perimeter members, purchased locking casters; reduce the level count/pitch for the actual projects.
6. **Print only the mechanical intelligence.** Pan clips, corner gussets, caster interfaces, and spacing/drill jigs. Use real half sheets as trays and optionally as the top deck.
7. **Fail closed on fits.** Measure one pan, structural stock, and caster interface before production. Print one clip and one caster shoe first; only then duplicate the batch.
8. **Rate honestly.** Do not inherit a commercial rack’s load rating. Progressively load-test the prototype on locked casters and publish only the tested working load.

This gate captures the durable workflow correction: a rejected buy price can justify a cart, but not a multi-kilogram segmented PETG furniture frame.
